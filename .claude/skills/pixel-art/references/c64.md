# The skill on the C64 (this game's art)

The art is text grids in `content/art/` (`docs/art.md`): figures, effects and tiles,
edited in the art desk (`make artdesk`) or by hand, checked by `python3 tools/art.py
check`, built by `tools/battlegfx.py`. `art.py` refuses anything the C64 can't show, so
the rules below are about what looks good inside what it can.

## What the hardware allows

- **Figures** (`figures.txt`): a multicolour sprite, 12 dots x 21 rows, each dot two
  pixels wide. Four values: `.` see-through, `a` and `b` the two colours every sprite
  shares (the file's `shared`: now dgrey, lgrey), `c` the figure's own. So a figure has
  **three colours**, two of them the same as every other figure's. Feet on the bottom row.
  The C version adds a black hires outline round the body (`outline`); the cartridge has
  no sprite for it, so it draws the outline into the body in shared colour 1, which it
  makes black (`outlined`); the walk engine does the same.
- **Effects** (`effects.txt`): hires (24 x 21, one colour) or multicolour like a figure.
- **Tiles** (`tiles32*.txt` 16 x 32 dots, `tiles16*.txt` 8 x 16): multicolour cells. Four
  values: `k` the background, `d` `g` the two shared, `o` the tile's own, **one of the
  first eight colours** (black white red cyan purple green blue yellow). A look's
  background and shared colours are three registers for the whole screen. A tiles16
  look holds at most 21 tiles.

## The skill's rules, translated

- **Palette and ramps.** No hue-shifted ramps of your own: the 16 colours are fixed. A
  ramp is a walk up the C64's brightness order, which pairs colours of equal lightness
  (on the usual palettes): black · blue, brown · red, dgrey · purple, orange · grey,
  lblue · green, lred(pink) · cyan, lgrey · yellow, lgreen · white. Steps of one or two
  groups read as shading; two colours of the same group side by side read as one
  colour (useful for mixing, useless for edges). Warm-to-cool shading still works in
  the order: brown → orange → yellow for lit warm surfaces, blue → purple → lblue for
  cool shade.
- **Choose the shared colours first.** On a figure, `a` and `b` are every figure's: they're
  the clothes, metal, shadow and outline-adjacent tones of the whole cast, so pick them
  to serve as the cast's common ramp (dgrey and lgrey give a neutral one) and spend `c`,
  the only free colour, on what tells a figure apart (skin, coat, glow).
- **Silhouette first.** At 12 double-wide dots, the silhouette is the figure. Fill it
  solid: does it still say which race or foe? Distinct heads, shoulders, weapons and
  stance count for more than any inner detail. Keep the two sides' silhouettes
  different enough that facing reads when mirrored.
- **No 1-dot limbs where you can help it** (a dot is already two pixels wide, so one dot
  is the floor: "thin protrusions" means one row tall too). Legs and arms of a dot each
  are fine; a lone dot sticking out reads as noise.
- **Outlines.** The black outline is the build's, not the art's: don't draw one into the
  figure (the cartridge would double it). Selective outlining isn't open to the art; light
  the body instead (the lighter shared colour on the side toward the light, top left).
- **Light from the top left**, the same on every figure, tile and effect. No pillow
  shading (darkening every edge).
- **Dithering**: not on figures (too small). On tiles, one pattern a look, and only where
  an area is big enough to show it (a tiles32 tile's open ground, water), never on
  tiles16 detail; checkerboards of two same-brightness colours read as a flat mix,
  which is the C64's honest extra colour.
- **Tiles must tile.** Every terrain tile is drawn next to itself and its variants: draw
  the edges to meet (test a 3 x 3 of it, and next to `open`), keep big shapes off the
  edges, and give a floor two or three variants so it isn't one tile repeated.
- **Readable at size.** Judge at 1x on the C64's screen (320 x 200, double-wide dots), not
  zoomed in the desk: the desk's "as the game shows it" previews are the test.
- **Frames**: a figure has one pose now (and the build's fallen one). When animation
  comes: 2 frames for idle, 4 for a walk, timed to the step (no sliding feet).
