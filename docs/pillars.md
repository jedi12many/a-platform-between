# Pillars

Five ideas. If a feature doesn't serve one of them, it waits.

## 1. Bite-sized novellas

Each **Departure** is a complete story in 4–6 hours, about five chapters, one strong hook,
cheap to buy. Departures release in seasons: each stands alone, with a thread running
through them. Most are solo; some are written for parties of 2–4. We start with solo.

Seasons are the business model: Season 1 (the Waystation) is the base game, each later
season is a DLC with a new station, Stationmaster and main story, and everything you've
done carries over. Any Departure can be replayed for a different outcome.
See [seasons.md](seasons.md). Between Departures there are **Sidings**: procedural dungeons
and mini-games that build your character. See [sidings.md](sidings.md).

## 2. One character, every world

You make a character once and take them everywhere. Level, gear, Debt and Echoes travel
in the **Passport**: a password on retro machines, a file or an account on modern ones.

## 3. Translation

Every realm has a Tech Level and a Magic Level. When you arrive, your gear and even your
body change form to fit, but keep their power. A pulse rifle becomes a crossbow of
burning bolts. You can refuse (**Force It**) and pay in **Dissonance**.
See [translation.md](translation.md).

## 4. Echoes: your choices follow you

A few key choices in each Departure are recorded. Later Departures, earlier eras and
other realms remember them. Save the dire wolf puppy, and a grown wolf answers your call
mid-fight. Let the bandit go, and he's a warlord three Departures later.
See [echoes.md](echoes.md).

## 5. Rules that fit on a Commodore 64

Whole numbers, every stat under 255, identical dice everywhere. If the rules run on a
1 MHz 8-bit machine, they run on anything, and the same fight plays out the same way on
every platform. See [rules-v0.md](rules-v0.md).

## Tone

Weird science-fantasy that borrows from every genre. Strange, sometimes funny, sometimes
sad, never cynical. Choices have consequences in both directions; there is no
good/evil meter.
