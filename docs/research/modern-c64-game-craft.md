# Polish, not tricks, makes modern C64 games

Modern Commodore 64 games get their modern feel mostly from discipline, not exotic hardware tricks. They use cheap raster splits and luma-aware palettes. They play tracker music through a small, well-defined player that hands one voice to sound effects. Their loaders detect the drive and fall back gracefully. Their art is made in editors that enforce the hardware's rules. And the releases are finished, patched, and clear about the hardware they need. The showpiece techniques, such as FLI and NUFLI pictures, VSP scrolling and side-border opening, belong to demos and title screens. In gameplay they cost the whole CPU, flicker, or crash some machines. For *A Platform Between* this is good news. Its screen design (a multicolour bitmap picture over a hires text window, and a character-mode battle map with multiplexed two-sprite figures) already matches what the evidence recommends. The largest remaining gaps are the ones players notice most: music, transitions, and load handling for SD2IEC. The cheapest wins are a GoatTracker-style player kept resident in the main program, with tune data loaded per chapter and effects stealing one voice; luma-table fades; an ELoad path beside the KERNAL path, with saves kept on the KERNAL; and a battle interface that fixes Pool of Radiance's keystroke-heavy input. One caveat applies to everything below. **Every researcher reported that direct page fetches were blocked by the egress proxy.** The findings therefore rest on search-engine summaries of the cited pages, except the GoatTracker 2 readme, which was read in full from a mirror. Numbers flagged as unverified should be checked against the primary source before anyone relies on them.

## How far these sources can be trusted

The notes behind this report come from five research tracks: graphics, sound, loading, toolchains and notable games. In every track the proxy refused sites central to C64 development, among them csdb.dk, codebase64.org, c64-wiki.com, cadaver.github.io, linusakesson.net, itch.io and sd2iec.de. A claim cited to those pages below means "a search engine summarised the page as saying this", not "the page was read". Three kinds of numbers deserve the most suspicion. The first is cycle and rastertime figures for players, loaders and fades: **no primary source gave rastertime or size for any music player**, and the per-tune estimates below are background knowledge. The second is compiler benchmark tables, where the cc65 column was copied from a 2022 article rather than rerun. The third is release dates and version numbers seen only in snippets. Where the report gives a figure without a primary read, it says so. Where it states its own judgement about this project, it labels that judgement as such.

## Cheap raster work and luma-aware colour define the modern look

### Expensive modes are for title screens, not play

The modern C64 graphics toolkit is split sharply between cheap and expensive techniques, and shipped games stay almost entirely on the cheap side. Standard multicolour bitmap (160x200, four colours per 4x8 cell) costs no CPU to display. **FLI forces a badline on every raster line**, which leaves the CPU about four cycles per line, enough for exactly one store to $D011 ([Codebase64](https://codebase64.c64.org/doku.php?id=base%3Aintroduction_to_vertical_tweaks); [cobbpg](https://cobbpg.github.io/articles/nuflix.html)). It also loses the first three character columns to the "FLI bug" ([odd.blog](https://odd.blog/wp-content/blogs.dir/2/files/2008/01/intro-to-programming-c64-demos.html)). NUFLI reaches 320x200 with three colours per 8x2 area by layering sprites under AFLI. It cannot run full-width FLI with all sprites enabled, and the researchers found no use of it in interactive game screens ([C64-Wiki: NUFLI](https://www.c64-wiki.com/wiki/NUFLI); [cobbpg](https://cobbpg.github.io/articles/nuflix.html)). Interlaced modes such as IFLI flicker on CRTs and smear motion on LCDs ([Lemon64](https://www.lemon64.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=14376&sid=70728d478ea360d461343b6502fbcfa4)).

On the scrolling side, VSP gives full-colour scrolling (Mayhem in Monsterland, Creatures), but it **"doesn't work on all C64s; on some it will crash"** ([Lemon64](https://www.lemon64.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=81818)). Opening the side borders needs a cycle-exact write on every line, which effectively consumes the CPU ([Antimon](https://www.antimon.org/dl/c64/code/opening.txt)). Opening the top and bottom borders costs one $D011 write per frame. FLD, which delays badlines, is cheap enough that Parallaxian uses it in gameplay for vertical parallax ([Kodiak64](https://kodiak64.co.uk/blog/vertical-parallax-on-commodore-64)).

### What carries the look in released games

The acclaimed releases owe their look mainly to art direction. Galencia's graphics are described as "of a caliber more often associated with the C64 demo scene than C64 games" ([C64-Wiki](https://www.c64-wiki.com/wiki/Galencia)). Caren and the Tangled Tentacles won best graphics at the Forum64 Adventure Compo with plain **multicolour character-mode rooms, 40x17 characters**, leaving the rest of the screen to its verb and inventory interface ([RGCD diary](https://www.rgcd.co.uk/2016/10/developer-diary-caren-and-tangled.html); [Indie Retro News](https://indieretronews.com/2015/10/caren-and-tangled-tentacles-v11-winner.html)). Briley Witch Chronicles, the one widely praised modern C64 RPG, adds sprite portraits for each NPC in dialogue, "extra charsets to add more detail", and enough bitmaps that it moved to EasyFlash to hold them ([VITNO](https://www.vintageisthenewold.com/c64-briley-witch-chronicles); [itch.io](https://sarahjaneavory.itch.io/briley-witch-chronicles)).

Colour practice is luma-driven. Later VIC-II revisions put the 16 colours into nine luminance levels, and the earliest revisions into five ([Pepto via unusedino.de](http://unusedino.de/ec64/technical/misc/vic656x/colors/); [ilesj](https://ilesj.wordpress.com/2016/03/30/old-vic-ii-colors-and-color-blending/)). On PAL the TV's delay line blends adjacent lines, so equal-luma colour pairs dither cleanly and large luma gaps show as noise ([pagetable.com](https://www.pagetable.com/c64ref/colors/)). Naive nearest-colour conversion gives "rather ugly images" ([Dig-Art Roks](https://www.digartroks.be/img2c64mc/)).

### Sprites on the battle screen

Modern multiplexers sort virtual sprites by Y, often with Cadaver's continuous insertion sort, which keeps last frame's order so a nearly sorted list re-sorts cheaply. They double-buffer the sorted table and rewrite the eight hardware sprites from raster IRQs set just above each reuse ([Lemon64](https://www.lemon64.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=41494&sid=45b6b2e7b292c18bcbdcfe81327b38f3); [selmiak](http://selmiak.bplaced.net/games/c64/index.php?lang=eng&game=Tutorials&page=Sprite-Multiplexing)). Hessian runs a **24-sprite multiplexer** with real-time depacking through a sprite cache ([Hessian README](https://github.com/cadaver/hessian)). Cadaver's rule is that raster IRQs should set VIC registers and play music, and nothing else ([Cadaver](https://cadaver.github.io/rants/interp.html)). A hires sprite laid over a multicolour body is described as "quite common" in games. The cost is a second hardware sprite per figure ([Lemon64](https://www.lemon64.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=1092857)).

### What transfers to A Platform Between

The project already has the recommended structure. Its multicolour bitmap picture window plus raster split to a hires text window in a custom font is the cheap, CPU-free layout the graphics research arrives at. The 40-column custom font is the right call: 4x8 "soft-80" glyphs are close to illegible ([Imran Nazar](https://imrannazar.com/Extended-Text-Mode-on-the-C64)). The two-sprite figures follow the common overlay practice. Their cost is concrete: with eight hardware sprites, **at most four figures can share a raster band**, so the existing Y-sorted multiplexer is load-bearing.

In a turn-based battle the sort only needs to run when something moves. The C64-Wiki's sprite DMA figure, up to 19 stolen cycles per line with all eight sprites active, is a reason to keep the number of IRQs low. One forum suggestion is to space them roughly every eighth line ([C64-Wiki](https://www.c64-wiki.com/wiki/raster_time); [CSDb](https://csdb.dk/forums/?roomid=11&topicid=63220&showallposts=1)).

The clear gap is **transitions**. A fade steps each colour to its next-darker luma neighbour through a 16-entry table. That has to be applied to colour RAM, screen RAM and $D020/$D021 every few frames. A fade-in needs separate per-colour ramps up from black, because the fade-out table does not invert uniquely ([Lemon64](https://www.lemon64.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=21270&sid=c1111ac7ce238621ff034e15cbd1642e)). The fade table posted there (00,0D,0B,05,02,0A,09,0F,04,00,0C,06,0E,07,08,03) was itself labelled unverified. This project should derive its own table from the cited luma order and test it with the Python picture tools, not copy the forum's. The researchers' estimate of 10–15k cycles per full-screen colour-RAM step is unverified.

Moving the raster split line frame by frame gives a "curtain" wipe over the picture window at almost no cost. Opening the top and bottom borders is a cheap place for decoration. FLI, NUFLI, VSP and side-border opening should stay out of gameplay. A full-screen FLI "chapter plate" shown while the game waits for a keypress is the only place the expensive modes fit. That is the researchers' suggestion, not an observed practice. Art should be checked on both 9-luma and 5-luma palettes, since red, blue, brown and dark grey collapse to a single level on the earliest machines ([ilesj](https://ilesj.wordpress.com/2016/03/30/old-vic-ii-colors-and-color-blending/)).

## GoatTracker's three-call interface fits an overlay game best

Of the modern trackers, **GoatTracker 2 is the one documented for game integration**, and it was the only primary source the sound researcher read in full. Its interface has three calls. Init is `LDA #subtune / JSR start`. Play is `JSR start+3` once per frame. A sound effect starts with `LDA #<fx / LDY #>fx / LDX #channel / JSR start+6`. An optional volume entry point takes 0–15 for fades ([GT2 readme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leafo/goattracker2/master/readme.txt)).

The packer/relocator strips every pattern, instrument and piece of player code a song does not use, so player size depends on the song. A "zeropage ghostregs" option writes SID state to zero page and lets the game copy it to $D400–$D418 itself. That lets music and player live under I/O, and it lets a program read the 25 register bytes after each call ([GT2 readme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leafo/goattracker2/master/readme.txt)). One song file can hold several subtunes, and a "timing mark" command lets game code sync to musical cues. SID Factory II, with drivers by Laxity and JCH, is the main newer editor and has its own packer, but its page gives no driver sizes or rastertime ([SID Factory II](https://github.com/Chordian/sidfactory2)).

**No source gave rastertime figures for any player.** GoatTracker's readme only says to measure the example programs, "No promises!" The researcher's estimate of 10–25 raster lines per frame and 1–2 KB of player code for a typical game tune is unverified background knowledge.

### Sound effects take a voice from the music

Music and effects share the chip by channel stealing. An effect takes one voice, the music on that voice is muted, and it resumes when the effect ends. GoatTracker builds in a priority rule: **an effect at a higher memory address is never interrupted by one at a lower address**. Its effect format is tiny: attack/decay, sustain/release, a nybble-swapped pulse byte, then note/waveform pairs ended by $00, with converted effects capped at 128 bytes ([GT2 readme](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/leafo/goattracker2/master/readme.txt)). One documented artefact matters with ghostregs: an effect's pulse width can linger into the next music note if that note does not set its own. 4mat's "SFX Anywhere" adds the same steal-and-restore behaviour to players that lack it ([itch.io](https://4mat.itch.io/soundfx-anywhere)).

### Chip revisions and samples

The two SID revisions differ mainly in the filter and the output bias. The 6581's filter is non-linear and varies from chip to chip. The 8580's is close to spec, and the 8580 nearly silences the classic $D418 volume-register digis ([CPCWiki](https://cpcwiki.eu/index.php/SID)). Modern high-quality sample playback, such as Mahoney's 2014 *Musik Run/Stop*, is demo technique ([Hugi 38](https://www.hugi.scene.org/online/hugi38/hugi%2038%20-%20demoscene%20reports%20magic%20the%20making%20of%20musik%20run%20stop.htm)). For an RPG that loads from disk, digis cost too much CPU and memory and behave differently on each chip.

### Music in classic and modern RPGs

Classic C64 RPGs used little music. The C64-Wiki says The Bard's Tale had only one-voice title music plus the bard songs ([C64-Wiki](https://c64-wiki.com/wiki/The_Bard's_Tale)). The claim that Pool of Radiance's C64 version had music rests only on a fan wiki ([Annex](https://annex.fandom.com/wiki/Pool_of_Radiance)). Modern releases treat sound as a selling point: Sam's Journey credits a separate "Sound & SFX" composer and tunes "that use all three voices" ([Protovision](https://www.protovision.games/games/sams_journey.php)), and Briley Witch players single out its music and effects ([itch.io review](https://itch.io/event/8277667)).

### What transfers to A Platform Between

The house rules shape the integration. Play is called from the raster IRQ, and overlays must never call one another, so **the player code belongs in the main program, and each chapter's tune data loads alongside it**, with subtunes for travel, tension, battle and victory or defeat stingers. Effects can use GoatTracker's effect format and channel argument instead of the current one-voice routine. That gives address-ordered priority for free: put a critical hit above a menu blip. Because the format is just ADSR, a pulse byte and note/wave pairs, a Python reference can produce or check it byte for byte, in line with the rule that golden values are derived independently.

Transitions between scenes can fade the master volume through the volume entry point, then init the next subtune. Silence on a story beat costs nothing. One question must be tested rather than assumed: whether music keeps steady time while the KERNAL is loading. Demo IRQ loaders exist precisely so music can play during loads, and the notes have no evidence either way for the KERNAL path.

On desktop and browser, the standard SID library, libsidplayfp with reSIDfp, is **GPL-2.0-or-later** ([libsidplayfp](https://github.com/libsidplayfp/libsidplayfp)). Browser options include WebSID, which wraps the Tiny'R'Sid player, and jsSID ([wothke/websid](https://github.com/wothke/websid); [jsSID](https://github.com/og2t/jsSID)). The project's own C SID-like synth offers a third path the notes did not consider. A small 6502 core could run the very same packed GoatTracker binary each frame and feed the 25 ghostreg bytes to that synth. That keeps the C64 and modern builds note-for-note identical and avoids the licence question. This is a project judgement. It depends on the synth modelling enough of the SID's envelope and waveform behaviour.

Licensing is simple: HVSC tunes are copyrighted and HVSC "cannot grant licences" ([HVSC FAQ](https://www.exotica.org.uk/wiki/High_Voltage_SID_Collection/FAQ)). Commission original music, get the `.sng` source along with the `.sid`, and agree in writing on the platforms (C64, desktop and browser) and the credit.

## SD2IEC rewards the KERNAL path plus ELoad

The four big demo IRQ loaders (Krill's, Spindle, Sparkle, Bitfire) are, by Krill's own possibly partial account, "all on par performance-wise" ([CSDb](https://csdb.dk/forums/?roomid=11&topicid=174465)). They are built for one 1541-class drive, speed and small size, and mostly not for saving ([Codebase64](https://codebase.c64.org/doku.php?id=base:fastloaders)).

SD2IEC is the deciding constraint. It runs no 6502 drive code. Instead it hashes whatever drive code a program uploads and switches to the protocol of a loader it recognises ([CSDb forum](https://csdb.dk/forums/?roomid=11&topicid=134919)). **Krill's loader runs on SD2IEC only through its slow KERNAL fallback**, unless the user has the thierer/sd2iec firmware fork, whose status in 2026 is unverified ([CSDb v192](https://csdb.dk/release/?id=220685&show=notes)). Saves that run custom code on the drive simply fail ([Lemon64](https://www.lemon64.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=43794)).

The pattern that fits a game comes from Cadaver. His loader detects the drive and picks one of three paths: its own fastloader, a KERNAL fallback, or **ELoad, a protocol built into SD2IEC firmware since 0.10.2** and switched on by sending the string "eload" ([c64gameframework](https://github.com/cadaver/c64gameframework/blob/master/README.md); [go4retro](https://www.go4retro.com/?p=692)). It addresses files by two-letter hex names and has save routines that can be left out ([cadaver/c64loader](https://github.com/cadaver/c64loader)).

### Compression

Exomizer and ByteBoozer 2 pack tightest but decrunch slower. TSCrunch is the fastest decoder at a worse ratio. In one undated comparison, Exomizer packed a sample to 6688 bytes and TSCrunch to 7632 ([StarDot](https://stardot.org.uk/forums/viewtopic.php?t=27861); [TSCrunch](https://github.com/staD020/TSCrunch)). Over a serial bus the transfer dominates, so ratio wins. From a cartridge or ELoad, decoder speed matters more.

### Cartridges

Cartridges are the modern answer to load times. Ultima IV Remastered "reduces the severity of the C64-port's loading time by putting everything on a cartridge" ([Ultima Codex wiki](https://wiki.ultimacodex.com/wiki/Ultima_IV_Remastered)), and Briley Witch and its sequel ship as EasyFlash images ([itch.io BWC2](https://sarahjaneavory.itch.io/briley-witch-chronicles-2-c64)). But cartridge saves are fragile on the hardware people actually own. On an Ultimate 64 or 1541U, Briley Witch's "Save To Cart" reports success and saves nothing. **Its author tells players to save to SD2IEC**, partly because updating the .crt wipes saves stored inside it ([itch.io](https://sarahjaneavory.itch.io/briley-witch-chronicles)). Kung Fu Flash 2 and the Chameleon also keep the flash in RAM until the user saves it by hand ([OasisBBS](https://theoasisbbs.com/kung-fu-flash-2-a-smarter-faster-upgrade-for-c64-users/)).

### What transfers to A Platform Between

The project's KERNAL LOAD and sequential files are the most portable base there is. JiffyDOS users get them faster automatically, and they already work on SD2IEC, Pi1541, Ultimate devices and real drives. The upgrade with the best return is an **ELoad path for SD2IEC added beside the KERNAL path**, following Cadaver's three-way detection. **Saves should stay on standard DOS commands**, never custom drive code. A 1541 fastloader is a lower priority for an SD2IEC-first audience.

The game should also keep save rotation itself. Pool of Radiance's reference card told players to alternate between two save disks by hand ([SSI card](https://mocagh.org/ssi/pool-refcard.pdf)). Numbered overlay and chapter files already resemble Cadaver's hex-named content.

A single .d81 would avoid disk swaps for drives that support it. The current multi-.d64 per-Departure layout is the safe lowest common denominator. An EasyFlash build, if one ever comes, needs disk saves and an explicit warning about cartridge emulators.

## cc65's speed gap is real, but profiling beats switching compilers

On every published comparison the researchers found, cc65 is the slowest maintained C compiler for the 6502. In C64-Wiki's rerun of the gglabs suite, **Dhrystone took 1.4 under Oscar64, 1.9 under llvm-mos and 10.0 under cc65**, and AES-256 took 12.5, 10.1 and 195.4 ([C64-Wiki: oscar64](https://www.c64-wiki.com/wiki/oscar64)). These are search-summary figures with unclear units, and the cc65 column was copied from the 2022 article rather than rerun. Code size is mixed: one LZG decoder came out at 696 bytes under llvm-mos against 501 under cc65 ([8bitworkshop](https://8bitworkshop.com/docs/posts/2023/llvm-mos-sdk-6502-c-compiler.html)). Oscar64 has the most visible list of shipped C64 games of any modern C compiler. It also has native overlays for d64, d71 and d81 images, and it imports CharPad `.ctm` and SpritePad `.spd` files directly ([oscar64](https://github.com/drmortalwombat/oscar64); [manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/drmortalwombat/oscar64/main/oscar64.md)).

The more useful finding for a cc65 project is ilmenit's step-by-step optimisation of a cc65 program, which cut it **from 528 to 29 PAL ticks without changing compilers**. The steps were: `-Osir -Cl`; globals instead of C-stack parameters; struct-of-arrays layout; lookup tables in place of calculations and switches (296 to 67 ticks); and intermediate variables to avoid integer promotion (67 to 34 ticks) ([ilmenit](https://github.com/ilmenit/CC65-Advanced-Optimizations)). cc65's optimizer bugs keep being fixed on git master after V2.19, including a 16-bit comparison miscompile ([cc65 mirror](https://git.applefritter.com/6502/cc65/commit/131f96eb1eda192623b1b3e323a2a0739645b641)). That matches the house rule to suspect the compiler when sim65 and native results differ.

Switching compilers would cost the project more than it gains. Neither Oscar64 nor llvm-mos is known to run under sim65, and both bring their own linkers and runtimes in place of the ca65/ld65 overlay configuration. llvm-mos's larger code also risks the 4 KB chapter limit. The better investments, in order, are these. First, a cycle profiler in the existing py65 or sim65 harness, using ld65 labels, so hot paths get the ilmenit treatment or move to ca65. Second, CharPad and SpritePad import in the Python tools, so artists can use the standard editors while `tools/test_pictures.py` stays authoritative. Third, a headless VICE smoke test, run with `-limitcycles` and `-exitscreenshot`, to compare real VIC-II output against `tools/vic.py` ([Thomas Powell](https://thomaspowell.com/2021/08/08/running-a-commodore-vice-emulator-on-a-remote-ubuntu-linux-machine-with-xfvb/); the flag behaviour was not verified against VICE's manual).

## Briley Witch and Pool of Radiance set the design targets

Few modern C64 RPGs have shipped. **Briley Witch Chronicles (2021) is the clearest success**, and it is small, charming and story-first. ZZAP! 64 called it an instant classic that filled a long-standing gap and praised a story that develops over more than a dozen hours without forced grinding. It also criticised frequent random encounters and tricky early battles ([ZZAP! 64](https://www.pressreader.com/uk/zzap-64-9YY3/20211207/281573769020015)). The game was self-published on itch.io at $9.99, patched after release, and playable with a one-button joystick ([itch.io](https://sarahjaneavory.itch.io/briley-witch-chronicles)).

The cautionary case is Unknown Realm. Its Ultima-scale Kickstarter launched in 2016 and has "slipped into near-legend" without shipping ([Oasis BBS](https://theoasisbbs.com/stirring-dragon-games-shares-new-track-from-unknown-realm/)). The researchers found **no modern Gold Box-style tactical RPG on the C64**, and could not confirm whether the original C64 Gold Box games drew combatants as sprites or characters.

Pool of Radiance shows what to keep and what to fix. Its rules hold up. A 2011 critique praised initiative order and a battlefield copied from the walls around the party ([wurb.com](https://www.wurb.com/stack/archives/702)), and a retro review credits its flanking, line of sight, morale and area spells ([textfiles](https://mirror.cyberbits.eu/textfiles.com/games/REVIEWS/poolrad.rev)). Its interface has not held up. The same critique faults the many keystrokes and odd key choices, and wants a single "done" input after moving. Bump-to-attack in Move mode already pointed the way ([wurb.com](https://www.wurb.com/stack/archives/702)). Players today still name the interface as the real hurdle ([Beamdog](https://forums.beamdog.com/discussion/comment/1155520)). The C64 version shipped on four double-sided disks with slow loads ([Annex](https://annex.fandom.com/wiki/Pool_of_Radiance)), and its later reference card added a universal Exit key ([SSI card](https://mocagh.org/ssi/pool-refcard.pdf)).

The polished action releases (Sam's Journey, Galencia, Steel Ranger, Hessian) share several habits. They finish the game and patch it after release. They state hardware needs plainly: Sam's Journey drew complaints because its NTSC disk version needs a REU that was not clearly listed ([Protovision itch.io](https://protovision.itch.io/sams-journey)). They pair a cheap download with a deluxe physical edition. Steel Ranger's box held a printed manual, a map and a soundtrack CD ([Psytronik](https://psytronik.itch.io/steelranger)). RGCD bundled VICE with the Rocket Smash EX download ([RGCD](https://www.rgcd.co.uk/2015/01/rocket-smash-ex-available-commodore-64.html)).

## Conclusion

The research turns the question "how do we look modern?" into "what do we still owe the player?" The project's screen architecture is already the modern consensus, so no rewrite toward demo-grade modes is needed. What separates acclaimed modern releases from merely competent ones is the layer the project has not built yet: a soundtrack with stingers and fades, scene transitions, interface comfort, and loading that respects the player's hardware. Each of these is cheap in cycles and expensive only in attention. Running the C64's own music binary in the desktop and browser builds is a natural extension of the project's crosscheck discipline. It would make "sounds the same everywhere" a testable property, as "plays the same everywhere" already is.

Two open questions deserve primary-source work before decisions are locked in. The first is real rastertime for a packed GoatTracker or SID Factory II tune measured in the project's own harness, together with whether music survives KERNAL loads. The second is the actual speed of ELoad on current SD2IEC firmware. Both can be answered by measurement rather than reading, which suits a project that already trusts sim65, py65 and its own Python references more than it trusts the internet.
