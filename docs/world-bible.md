# World bible

## The hook

Every traveler at the Waystation died. A wasteland scavenger mid-explosion. An elf knight
on a pike. A robot as it was melted for scrap. One heartbeat before the end, a hand reached
through reality and pulled them out.

They wake in the **Waystation**, a run-down, beautiful train station wedged into a crack
between worlds. Its keeper, the **Stationmaster**, explains the deal kindly:

> "You were dead. Now you're not. That's a service, and services are billed. Work off your
> fare, and I'll put you back exactly where I found you — with one small change. You'll
> survive."

Every Departure is a **ticket**: a contract that pays down your **Debt** (your fare).
Pay it all and you can go home and rewrite your own death.

## How the hook covers the game's needs

| Need | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why such odd company? | The Stationmaster recruits from every era and world. |
| Why short stories? | They're jobs. In, do the work, out. |
| Why a hub? | You live at the Waystation and can't leave except by train. |
| Translation | You don't belong in any realm, so each one disguises you to fit. |
| Keeping gear and levels | You're anchored to the Waystation. What you bring back is yours. |
| Death without permadeath | Die on a job and you're pulled out again, for a lot more Debt. |
| Solo and party jobs | Most tickets need one traveler; big ones need a crew. |
| Online hub | The other drinkers at the bar are other players' characters. |

## The Waystation

- **The concourse**: the departure board (the main menu), ticket window, lost-and-found.
- **The platforms**: each realm leaves from its own platform. The retro editions *are*
  platforms: "The C64 train leaves from Platform 6."
- **The bar** (name TBD): gossip, rumors, other travelers, Echo reactions.
- **Traders**: buy, sell and re-translate gear.
- **The ledger room**: where the Stationmaster keeps every traveler's account.

## The Stationmaster

Polite, tall, velvet coat, unsettling. Never lies, rarely explains. Has opinions about
your choices, and some of them change your Debt.

## Why "A" Platform Between

There are other stations. Later seasons can bring rival stations with their own
Stationmasters and different terms, travelers who jumped their fare, and, online, other
stations as separate hubs or factions.

## The mystery, in three layers

1. **Surface**: work off your Debt and go home.
2. **Underneath**: the tickets are odd. Steal a bell. Make sure a king dies on schedule.
   Plant a seed in 1,000,000 BC. Who benefits? Some travelers think the Stationmaster is
   fixing something, or covering something up. Factions form.
3. **Bottom**: worlds are fading. The **Static** erases realities no one remembers. The
   Waystation is an archive, the last place keeping those worlds alive, and travelers are
   its maintenance crew. Every visit keeps a world alive a little longer.
   (The retro platforms are realities kept alive by being played. Our real-world
   preservation project is the story's secret.)

## The endgame: going home

When Debt hits zero, choose:

- **Go home.** The character retires with a short custom epilogue of the death they now
  survive. They become a legend at the Waystation and may appear as an NPC in later
  Departures (see [Echoes](echoes.md)), with a bonus for your next character.
- **Stay.** Become a permanent traveler: deeper tickets, faction politics, party raids.

## Playable races (first pass)

| Race | Concept | Translation flavor |
|---|---|---|
| **Hollowborn** | An empty suit of armor animated by a contract. No body to heal; it gets repaired. | High tech: a drone shell. High magic: a haunted cuirass. |
| **Glassfolk** | Crystalline people who store memories as light. Hard hits can cost skills. | Low tech: carved quartz idols. |
| **Rad-Dryads** | Tree spirits mutated by fallout; they photosynthesize radiation. | High magic: classic dryads. |
| **Chronomites** | Small creatures slightly out of sync with time; they act a split second early. | Everywhere: "lucky." |
| **Salvaged** | A robot that believes it's a knight, and is technically correct. | High magic: a golem. |
| **Moth-folk of the Lantern Courts** | Drawn to light, aristocratic, fragile. | High tech: bio-engineered nobility. |
| **Human** | Any era, any world. The baseline everyone else is measured against. | Clothing and accent change. |
