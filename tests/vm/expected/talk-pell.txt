A camp in the middle of the room: a tarpaulin strung between two signal posts, a stove, and a woman sitting very still on a crate of tinned peaches. She has a ledger on her knee and a crossbow across it.
"Vell," she says. "I trade. I don't fight, and I don't lend." She looks you over. "First time down? Then here's the first thing free: the stairs are always in the room furthest from where you came in. Something always sits on them."
The courier behind you sees the stove and makes a small sound, like a kettle.
"Oh, you found the courier." Vell stands up for the first time. "Sit. Eat. Nobody eats down here unless I say so." The courier sits, and eats, and is asleep before the tin is empty.
"Kind of you," Vell says to you, which sounds like it costs her.
The courier wakes long enough to press something into your hands: their own coat, patched at both elbows, chain sewn into the lining. "For the cold," they say, and are gone again.
